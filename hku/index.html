CORE THESIS

There is no universally efficient embodied model.
The right brain depends on its body, task, and hardware.

This observation motivates Embodied-DEOS, where embodied intelligence is treated as a joint design problem rather than a fixed model followed by post-hoc compression.

Brain

WAM Search — What should the robot predict?

Rather than assuming that future RGB/video prediction is always necessary, search for the most task-relevant world representation under a fixed computational budget:

RGB / latent / depth / flow / object state / contact / tactile × horizon × resolution × precision.

Compute

Bit / Runtime — How much computation should the robot spend?

Treat computation itself as an embodied resource:

precision × tokens × layers × action steps × runtime policy × hardware mapping.

This branch contains the central Embodied-DEOS search framework and the Compute-as-Action direction.

Body

Hand Search — What body and sensory interface should intelligence inhabit?

Move beyond designing policies for a fixed hand and study:

morphology × action representation × tactile sensing × control bandwidth × compute.

This branch includes embodiment-policy-compute co-search, dexterity bottleneck analysis, and coarse-to-fine multimodal tactile intelligence.

Unifying Research Question

Embodied intelligence therefore becomes a three-way co-design problem:

Brain  ×  Compute  ×  Body

or more concretely:

What to predict  ×  How much to compute  ×  What embodiment to control

The long-term goal is not simply to make an existing robot model smaller. It is to discover the smallest computational and physical structure sufficient for intelligent interaction.

These directions are designed to complement Prof. Yang's embodied intelligence agenda by building a reusable research stack spanning efficient foundation models, world models, tactile intelligence, and dexterous embodiment.

P0 — Embodied-DEOS
Flagship		 Given a robot, task distribution, and edge-compute budget, what should its embodied model + precision + runtime + hardware stack look like? Jointly search architecture × bit-width × embodied structure × runtime × chip/mapping instead of compressing a fixed VLA after training. DaDu-Corki, ISCA'25 already establishes embodied algorithm–architecture co-design; QVLA, ICLR'26 performs action-centric channel-wise {0,2,4,8,16}-bit allocation; QuantVLA, CVPR'26 performs training-free VLA PTQ; ActQuant'26 reaches sub-4-bit action-guided mixed precision and real UR3 deployment; VQVLA'26 couples motion-aware dynamic precision with a custom accelerator; SpecVLA'26 performs state-aware speculative inference with heterogeneous hardware; KERV, DAC'26 uses robot kinematics for speculative decoding and reports 27–37% acceleration. All current strong works manually choose one efficiency axis: quantization, speculative inference, vector quantization, or hardware acceleration, usually after the model architecture is fixed. Missing: task-aware joint search over the complete embodied stack under closed-loop success constraints. Start from pretrained OpenVLA-OFT / π0.5-like VLA. P1 — WAM-DEOS
What Should the Robot Predict?		 Current WAMs usually assume that predicting future RGB/video/latent video is desirable. Ask instead: under a fixed compute budget, what future representation is actually useful for control? Search prediction target × horizon × resolution × frequency × precision. Light-WAM'26 reduces WAM cost with a compact video backbone, downsampled latent supervision and lightweight StateFusionActionExpert; Q-WAM'26 pushes WAMs to W4A4 while protecting action-sensitive subspaces and evaluates on RoboTwin 2.0 and real robots. These works optimize how a predefined WAM is computed. Dexterous Tactile World Model'26 further shows that adding tactile force magnitude/location changes future prediction quality, reinforcing that representation choice matters. Missing question: why predict pixels at all? Prediction fidelity and downstream policy utility need not align. No systematic search currently decides whether a control task should predict RGB, latent features, depth, flow, object state, contact, tactile state or task features. Shared observation encoder + multiple lightweight prediction heads. Candidate targets: RGB / latent / depth / optical flow / object pose / contact map / tactile latent / policy feature. Search target + future horizon {1,2,4,8} + spatial resolution + bit. Evaluate downstream policy success, not just reconstruction. Full RGB future prediction; latent future prediction; Light-WAM; Q-WAM; no-WM VLA; same compute with different manually selected targets; random target search. LIBERO first, RoboTwin 2.0 second. Use state/contact ground truth from simulation where possible to cheaply construct candidate future targets. 2×4090 minimum; 2–4 A100/H100 comfortable. Avoid training a large video foundation model from scratch; freeze/pretrain backbone and train lightweight heads. W1: WAM/control baseline. W2: compare RGB vs latent vs state/contact targets. Killer plot = reconstruction quality vs control success. W3: add horizon/resolution search. W4: Pareto table. W5: RoboTwin. W6: second WAM/backbone. W7–8: ablation + writing. If target type differences are weak → search horizon × resolution × temporal frequency only. If generative WAM training is too expensive → freeze WM and search representation heads. If RGB/state labels are inconsistent → use intermediate pretrained feature prediction. 4/5 difficulty; 5/5 ceiling. Particularly attractive if a clear “pixels are not the best future representation” result appears. 5/5. Easy follow-ups for humanoids, driving, dexterous manipulation and navigation. Strong PhD + Master maintaining WAM benchmark/data pipeline. Builds a reusable world-model branch under Prof. Yang's agenda and makes the efficiency line relevant to one of the fastest-growing embodied-model paradigms.
P2 — Compute-as-Action A robot normally predicts physical action at. We additionally let it predict a computational action ct: how much computation should be spent now? π(ot) → (at, ct). ct can control bit-width, inference depth, action-generation steps, VLM invocation or speculative-verification budget. VQVLA'26 already adjusts vector-quantization precision based on robot motion state; SpecVLA'26 distinguishes active/inactive states and changes action/speculation strategy; KERV'26 dynamically adjusts speculative thresholds using kinematic variability; QVLA/ActQuant optimize precision based on action sensitivity but primarily offline. Dynamic compute itself is therefore not enough. The gap is to treat computation as a learned control variable jointly optimized with physical action, rather than a handcrafted state rule or offline allocation. Begin with a very small action space: ct ∈ {4,8,16} bit or {short, medium, full compute}. Train a scheduler from state/uncertainty/contact signal using task reward minus compute penalty. Later expand to token count / flow steps / VLM call frequency. Fixed FP16; fixed INT8/INT4; QVLA/ActQuant; VQVLA-style state heuristic; entropy/uncertainty heuristic; random scheduler; oracle budget from hindsight. LIBERO + ManiSkill. Choose tasks containing easy free-space phases and hard contact/precision phases so compute allocation has a clear temporal structure. 1–2×4090. No new robot required in first iteration. W1: fixed compute sweep. W2: locate state-dependent sensitivity. W3: train bit/computation scheduler. W4: learned vs heuristic. W5: second compute knob or benchmark. W6: ablation/write. If RL scheduler is unstable → supervised oracle/imitation scheduler. If learned scheduler does not beat heuristic → pivot to uncertainty-calibrated compute scheduling. If bits provide weak dynamic effect → use diffusion/flow steps or VLM invocation frequency. 3.5/5 difficulty; 4/5 ceiling. Conceptually clean and fast to test. 4.5/5. Can immediately branch into token-as-action, camera-rate-as-action, memory-as-action and world-model-steps-as-action. PhD or strong Master. Provides a fast, lower-risk companion paper to Embodied-DEOS and can directly supply the runtime policy used inside the flagship search space.
P3 — Embodied Information Budget Which sensory information deserves bandwidth at each stage of interaction? Instead of always processing RGB/depth/tactile/proprioception/history at maximum fidelity, dynamically allocate modality × sampling rate × tokens × precision. ET-Hand, ICRA'25 already learns task-specific multimodal tactile sensor placement and can adjust tactile positions/types/numbers, so “sensor placement search” alone is already occupied. TouchWorld'26 separates slow semantic planning from tactile prediction and fast tactile residual correction. TouchWGNN'26 fuses distributed tactile, vision and proprioception using spatiotemporal tactile graphs. EmArm'26 demonstrates the value of whole-arm tactile sensorimotor feedback. Existing works choose a sensing system or fusion architecture first. Missing: task-conditioned allocation of a unified sensory budget where sensing itself is treated as an efficiency resource: camera frames, tactile channels, history length, modality frequency and numerical precision. Build modality gates over RGB/depth/contact/tactile/proprio/history. Start with discrete choices: modality ON/OFF + sampling rate {low/high}. Then add token/bit budgets. Optimize task success minus sensor/compute bandwidth. Full multimodal policy; vision-only; tactile-only; fixed modality subsets; random modality dropout; fixed sampling rate; ET-Hand-style tactile placement where comparable; oracle modality mask. Start in ManiSkill / Isaac Lab contact-rich simulation, where contact/tactile proxies can be cheaply generated. Add real tactile stream only after a clear simulation signal. 1–2×4090. Real tactile hardware optional for first 4–6 weeks. W1: modality-mask sweep. W2: task-phase sensitivity map. W3: dynamic gate. W4: success vs sensory bandwidth. W5: rate/precision. W6: second task suite. If joint modality/rate/bit search is unstable → modality ON/OFF only. If simulated tactile is too artificial → frame project as visual/history bandwidth allocation first, then add touch later. 4/5 difficulty; 4/5 ceiling. 4.5/5. Especially relevant to sensor-rich humanoids where raw sensory bandwidth will become expensive. PhD; Master can own simulator/sensor instrumentation. Extends “efficiency” beyond model FLOPs into physical sensing cost, providing a bridge between Yang's embodied-efficiency and dexterous/tactile directions.
P4 — Embodiment–Policy–Compute Co-Search Do not do plain morphology–policy co-design. That problem is now directly occupied. Instead ask: what body, control representation and compute/sensing budget should be designed together for a task family? Search hand morphology × action representation × tactile layout × controller/compute budget. House of Dextra, ICLR'26 is a direct and very strong competitor: it already jointly searches task-specific hand morphology and control policy across finger/joint/palm structures and can fabricate/deploy discovered hands rapidly. Function-based Parametric Co-Design'26 further expands parametric hand geometry; CageCoOpt, IROS'25 jointly optimizes morphology and policy for caging robustness. On the representation side, XL-VLA, CVPR'26 and UniDex, CVPR'26 already tackle cross-hand action representations. Plain morphology-policy search is no longer novel enough. The open angle is co-designing the embodiment together with foundation-model action interface, tactile layout and computational/control bandwidth, ideally for a family of tasks rather than one task-specific hand. Restrict morphology initially to an existing parameterized hand: finger count / actuated DoFs / coupling / fingertip geometry. Jointly search latent action dimension, active tactile regions and policy compute. Objective: task-suite performance − hardware cost − compute cost. No physical fabrication in first paper iteration. House of Dextra; fixed LEAP/Allegro morphology; morphology-only search; policy-only search; fixed latent action representation; XL-VLA/UniDex-style latent/raw action where feasible. House-of-Dextra/open dexterous simulation tasks; ManiSkill/Isaac Gym dexterous tasks. Optional final physical validation on one modular/LEAP-style hand. 2–4×4090. Main cost is simulation engineering, not GPU. W1: fixed-hand baseline. W2: sweep DoF/coupling/latent-dim and identify interaction. W3: parameterized search. W4: joint vs separated optimization. W5: multi-task setting. W6: tactile/compute dimension. W7–8: generalization/write. If morphology simulation becomes a bottleneck → freeze morphology family and search DoF activation × coupling × action representation. If gains over House of Dextra are unclear → pivot to resource-constrained generalist hand design: one morphology under a budget across many tasks. 5/5 difficulty; 5/5 ceiling, but much higher implementation risk than P0. 5/5 if successful because follow-up naturally extends to humanoid limbs, grippers and whole-body design. Strong dexterous PhD; not the best first project for a Master. Gives the group a body-design counterpart to Embodied-DEOS, while avoiding direct duplication of House of Dextra by injecting efficiency, representation and sensing co-design.
P5 — Coarse2FineTouch
Adaptive Multimodal Tactile Intelligence		 Touch should not always be processed at maximum fidelity. Use cheap coarse contact perception by default and selectively activate fine-grained spatial/temporal tactile processing when contact uncertainty, slip or precision manipulation requires it. Search/learn coarse vs fine tactile representation × modality × rate × resolution. TouchWorld'26 already builds a predictive–reactive tactile hierarchy with vision-language planning, tactile world prediction and high-frequency residual correction. Dexterous Tactile World Model'26 shows that both force magnitude and spatial location improve future manipulation prediction. DexTouch-WM'26 learns action-conditioned visual+tactile dynamics from scalable human touch. ViTacFormer'25 learns cross-modal vision–tactile representations for long-horizon dexterous manipulation. TouchWGNN'26 explicitly models distributed tactile signals as spatiotemporal graphs. On sensing resolution, ICRA'25 Non-Planar Tactile Super-Resolution reconstructs high-resolution GelSight-like tactile patterns from low-resolution Xela readings; SuperTac'25 also tackles tactile super-resolution. ET-Hand'25 already optimizes sensor placement, so placement alone is not the novelty. There is already hierarchy, fusion and super-resolution separately. Missing: a control policy that treats tactile fidelity itself as an adaptive resource. Decide when binary contact/coarse force is enough and when to invoke high-resolution spatial tactile, higher temporal frequency or expensive visuo-tactile fusion. Three-level tactile stack: L0 contact/slip flags → L1 sparse/low-res taxels → L2 high-res tactile image/full graph. Router conditioned on visual uncertainty, contact transition, tactile entropy and task phase. Train jointly or distill from a full-fidelity teacher. Optional second axis: visual+tactile cross-modal gating. Full high-resolution tactile policy; low-res tactile only; binary contact only; vision-only; fixed visuo-tactile fusion; ViTacFormer; TouchWGNN-style fusion; tactile SR then policy; random coarse/fine routing; oracle router from error/failure. Fast representation experiment: ICRA'25 Xela↔GelSight tactile-SR dataset. Multimodal: ViTaDex vision–tactile dataset. Manipulation: simulated LEAP/Isaac contact stream initially; real Xela/GelSight/LEAP if available. 1–2×4090 for first stage. Hardware version: LEAP/other hand + Xela/GelSight-type sensors. Avoid making custom tactile hardware before ML signal is established. W1: reproduce coarse/full tactile baselines. W2: quantify which manipulation states actually need fine tactile. W3: router MVP. W4: success vs tactile bandwidth/resolution. W5: multimodal vision+tactile router. W6: second task. W7–8: optional real-hand demo + writing. If dynamic router does not improve success/compute tradeoff → frame as coarse-to-fine tactile distillation. If real high-res tactile is unavailable → use low-res→high-res paired tactile data and prove representation efficiency first. If routing is trivial → switch to uncertainty-triggered tactile super-resolution where high-res is reconstructed only on difficult contacts. 4/5 difficulty; 5/5 ceiling if a real-hand demo is strong. Visually compelling and easy to explain. 5/5. Natural follow-ups: tactile event cameras, multimodal sensor scheduling, tactile chips, humanoid skin, world-model touch budgets. Strong tactile/dexterous PhD + Master on tactile data/representation. This is a very direct way to strengthen Prof. Yang's dexterous-hand topic with a modern multimodal/tactile angle, while simultaneously connecting it back to the group's efficiency theme.
P6 — Dexterity Bottleneck
Control-Bandwidth Scaling		 Rather than inventing another universal latent action space, ask a more scientific question: what is the minimum control bandwidth required for a given level of dexterity? Estimate task-dependent intrinsic action dimensionality D*(task) and how it scales with contact/task complexity. UniDex, CVPR'26 provides 50K trajectories across 8 hands with 6–24 DoFs and proposes FAAS; XL-VLA, CVPR'26 learns a shared cross-hand latent action space and scales pooled cross-hand demonstrations; High-DoF VLA Post-Training'26 learns a temporal hand-action codec and uses it throughout SFT, DAgger and residual RL. Cross-embodiment world-model work is also beginning to search embodiment-invariant representations. “Learn a compact latent action representation” alone is crowded. Missing is a systematic measurement/scaling law relating task/contact complexity to the minimum action information rate needed for success. This can produce a reusable scientific result rather than another codec. Train/compress action representations at latent dims D ∈ {2,4,8,16,32} or varying temporal bitrate. Evaluate raw joint / PCA / AE / VQ / learned temporal codec. Estimate minimum D achieving ≥95% of full-control success. Correlate D* with contact transitions, number of active fingers, object DoF and task horizon. Extend to dynamic task-conditioned latent dimension if static scaling exists. Raw joints; PCA; autoencoder; VQ/VQ-VAE; fixed latent dimension; XL-VLA-style shared latent; UniDex FAAS; temporal hand-action codec; random joint subset. UniDex dataset is particularly attractive because it already spans 8 hand embodiments and 6–24 DoFs. Supplement with simulated dexterous tasks to obtain clean contact/task-complexity annotations. 1–2×4090. This is one of the cheapest dexterous directions because it can start entirely offline. W1: representation baselines. W2: task × latent-dimension curves. W3: correlate with contact/task complexity. W4: first scaling law / kill decision. W5: second embodiment/data source. W6: dynamic latent extension or writing. If no clean universal scaling law appears → pivot to task-conditioned adaptive latent dimension. If latent dimension alone is too crude → measure action bitrate = dimension × temporal rate × precision instead. If representation dominates results → turn into a principled action-compression paper. 3.5/5 difficulty; 4.5/5 ceiling. Very low cost relative to potential scientific impact. 4.5/5. A good scaling law is likely to be cited by later hand/VLA papers even if they do not adopt the exact method. PhD or strong Master. Gives Prof. Yang's dexterous direction a fundamental/scientific thread, complementing the larger engineering-heavy tactile and morphology projects.

Recommended Priority

The projects should not be launched with equal priority.

Tier A — Immediate group-building priorities

  1. Embodied-DEOS — central flagship and shared efficiency methodology.
  2. Coarse2FineTouch — strongest bridge between Prof. Yang's dexterous-hand topic and the efficiency agenda.
  3. WAM-DEOS — high-upside foundation-model direction that can reuse the same search methodology.

Tier B — Fast parallel projects

  1. Compute-as-Action — low-cost and likely to produce a result quickly.
  2. Dexterity Bottleneck — cheap, scientific and suitable for a student to validate within weeks.

Tier C — Longer-term expansion

  1. Embodied Information Budget — becomes more valuable once tactile/sensor infrastructure is available.
  2. Embodiment–Policy–Compute Co-Search — high ceiling, but should only start after carefully differentiating from House of Dextra and building stable dexterous simulation infrastructure.

Key Positioning

The projects are deliberately connected rather than independent:

  • Embodied-DEOS asks what computation should an embodied model use?
  • WAM-DEOS asks what future should the robot model?
  • Compute-as-Action asks when should the robot spend computation?
  • Information Budget asks what sensory information is worth acquiring?
  • Coarse2FineTouch asks when does manipulation require fine physical perception?
  • Dexterity Bottleneck asks how much control bandwidth is fundamentally necessary?
  • Embodiment–Policy–Compute Co-Search asks what body and brain should be built together?

Together, they provide a systematic path from efficient computation → efficient sensing → dexterous interaction → embodied co-design, while sharing benchmarks, models, hardware profiling tools and student infrastructure.
